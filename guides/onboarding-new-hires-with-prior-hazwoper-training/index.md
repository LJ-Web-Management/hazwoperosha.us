# Onboarding New Hires With Prior HAZWOPER Training

Employer guide · 2 min read · Updated 2026-10-30

Experienced hires often arrive with a HAZWOPER certificate from a previous employer or training provider. You don't have to retrain them from scratch, but you do have to check what they bring and fill the gaps.

## Accepting prior training

Under 29 CFR 1910.120(e)(9), an employer who can show by documentation or certification that a worker's experience or training is equivalent to the required initial training doesn't have to repeat that initial training. Two conditions stay:

- Keep the documentation and give the worker a copy on request.
- Workers new to a site still need **site-specific training before entry** and appropriate **supervised field experience at the new site**.

## Verification checklist

1. **Certificate**: name, level, hours, regulatory basis, completion date and provider.
2. **Level matches the job**: a 24-hour certificate doesn't cover a general site worker role; they need the additional 16 hours and 2 days, or the full [40 hour course](https://www.hazwoperosha.us/courses/osha-40-hour-hazwoper-training/).
3. **Refresher is current**: within the last 12 months.
4. **Supervisor training** if they'll supervise: [OSHA 8 Hour HAZWOPER Supervisor Training](https://www.hazwoperosha.us/courses/osha-8-hour-hazwoper-supervisor-training/).
5. **State version** if needed, for example Cal/OSHA for California work.
6. **Provider check**: contact the issuer if anything looks off.

## If the refresher has lapsed

OSHA doesn't set a grace period. For a short lapse, most employers have the worker take the [8 hour refresher](https://www.hazwoperosha.us/courses/osha-8-hour-hazwoper-refresher-training/) before site work and record the employer's judgment that their initial skills are current. For a long lapse, or where skills can't be shown to be current, retake the initial course. Write your policy down and apply it the same way to everyone.

## First-week onboarding plan

| When | Task |
| --- | --- |
| Before day 1 | Collect and verify certificates; order any refresher or upgrade course |
| Day 1 | Company safety orientation; medical surveillance enrollment if their role requires it |
| Day 1-2 | Respirator medical evaluation and fit test on your models |
| Before site entry | Site-specific training: hazards, site plan, work zones, decon, emergency procedures |
| First site days | Supervised field experience with a buddy; sign-off by the supervisor |
| End of week | Training file complete; refresher due date in the tracking system |

## Hiring several people at once

Put every refresher, upgrade and new-hire course for the group in one cart. The [crew training planner](https://www.hazwoperosha.us/crew-training-planner/) builds the order by role.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I have to retrain a new hire who already has HAZWOPER?

Not the initial training, if you have documentation showing equivalent training under 1910.120(e)(9). They still need site-specific training and supervised field experience at your site, and a current annual refresher.

### What if a new hire's HAZWOPER refresher has expired?

Have them complete the 8 hour refresher before site work and document your judgment that their skills are current. After a long lapse, consider retaking the initial course.

### Is a 24 hour certificate enough for a general site worker?

No. General site workers need 40 hours. A 24-hour worker can upgrade with the additional 16 hours and 2 days of field experience, or take the full 40 hour course.

This guide summarizes federal OSHA, Cal/OSHA and EPA rules for training purposes. It is not legal advice. Always check the current regulation text and your site safety and health plan.
