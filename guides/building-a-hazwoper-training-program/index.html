How to Build a HAZWOPER Training Program for Your Company

A HAZWOPER training program is more than buying courses. It's a repeatable system that puts the right training in front of each worker before they need it, and proves you did. Here's how to build one.

Step 1: Decide whether and how HAZWOPER applies

  • Do employees work on cleanup sites, RCRA corrective actions or voluntary cleanups recognized by a government body? Then the site-worker rules in paragraph (e) apply.
  • Do they work at a permitted treatment, storage or disposal facility? Paragraph (p) applies.
  • Will employees respond to releases anywhere? Paragraph (q) applies, unless you evacuate and rely entirely on outside responders under an emergency action plan.
  • Check whether a state plan or EPA's Part 311 applies: see state plans and HAZWOPER.

Step 2: Build a role-to-training matrix

List every job title and decide its training from its duties and likely exposure. A simple matrix:

Occasional site worker24 Hour1 day supervised8 Hour Refresher
Site supervisor40 Hour + 8 Hour Supervisor3 days supervisedSupervisor Refresher
Employee who may discover a releaseFirst Responder AwarenessNoneRefresher or competency check
TSDF operator24 Hour TSDPer facility plan8 Hour TSD Refresher

The crew training planner does this math for you and builds the order.

Step 3: Plan field experience and site-specific training

Online courses cover the classroom portion. You still need to arrange supervised field days with a trained, experienced supervisor, and site-specific training before anyone enters a new site: the hazards, the site safety and health plan, work zones, decon and emergency procedures. Record both.

Step 4: Choose who trains

Trainers must be qualified by training or by academic credentials and instructional experience (1910.120(e)(5)). Most employers combine online courses for the classroom hours with in-house supervisors for field and site-specific training. Companies with steady hiring often certify an internal instructor with HAZWOPER Train-the-Trainer Certification.

Step 5: Put refreshers on a calendar

Track each worker's last training date and schedule the refresher a month before the anniversary. Ordering refreshers for a whole crew at once keeps everyone on the same cycle.

Step 6: Connect the other programs

Training sits alongside medical surveillance, respiratory protection (medical evaluation and annual fit testing) and the site safety and health plan. Line their schedules up so workers aren't pulled off the job three separate times.

Step 7: Budget and document

See budgeting for HAZWOPER training for a worked example, and training records for employers for what to keep.

Frequently asked questions

What should a HAZWOPER training program include?

A role-to-training matrix, initial training, supervised field experience, site-specific training, annual refreshers, qualified trainers, and records for every worker, coordinated with medical surveillance and respiratory protection.

Can online courses satisfy HAZWOPER training?

Online courses can cover the classroom portion. Employers still provide supervised field experience, hands-on practice with site equipment and site-specific training.

Who can be a HAZWOPER trainer?

Someone qualified by completing a training program for the subjects they teach, or by academic credentials and instructional experience, under 29 CFR 1910.120(e)(5).

This guide summarizes federal OSHA, Cal/OSHA and EPA rules for training purposes. It is not legal advice. Always check the current regulation text and your site safety and health plan.
